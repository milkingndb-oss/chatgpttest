# The Devouring Wolf Summoner

## Complete Rewritten Narration Draft

Before the world learned my name, it laughed at the creature in my arms.

That was the part everyone remembered later.

The tiny black puppy.

The ruined summoning ceremony.

The girlfriend who walked away because she thought my future had ended.

They liked to tell that part because it made the ending feel cleaner. A weak boy. A useless beast. A crowd of fools who failed to recognize a miracle.

But the truth was uglier than that.

No one laughed when Hey Ming stood over the ruins of Wooan, taller than the mountains, black flames dripping from his jaws as an army of mutant beasts burned beneath his shadow.

No one laughed when Diana split the sky with a crimson moon.

No one laughed when Silver Butterfly wrapped around me like living armor and the Fallen Clan warlord tried to retreat through a gate that Hey Ming was already devouring.

By then, the same people who once called my summon trash had begun calling him a disaster.

They were still wrong.

Disasters destroy without choosing.

Hey Ming chose.

He chose me when I had nothing left.

So if you want to understand how a palm-sized puppy became the beast that made alien kings afraid to open gates above our world, do not start with the battlefield.

Start with the empty room.

Start with the last coin my little sister pressed into my hand.

Start with the day everyone mistook loyalty for weakness.

The entire hall went quiet when my summoning circle faded.

For one breath, everyone stared at the thing sitting in the center of the platform. It was black, round-eyed, and small enough to fit in both hands. Its ears trembled. Its tail wagged once, as if it had no idea it had just ruined my future.

Then the laughter began.

"A puppy?"

"Chenlin awakened a pet?"

"No, not even a pet. That thing looks like it would lose to a house cat."

The voices struck from every direction. Some students covered their mouths, pretending to be polite. Others did not bother. Even Teacher Luen, who had once told the class I had the discipline to enter a top academy, looked as if someone had poured cold water over his hopes.

"Chenlin," he said through clenched teeth, "do you understand what this means? A summoner's first beast decides the width of his road. I spent months preparing you because I thought you had a chance."

I lowered my gaze to the little black puppy.

It looked up at me.

There was no fear in its eyes. No shame. Only a strange, steady loyalty, as if it had crossed a thousand storms to reach me and would not complain about the welcome.

So I laughed softly, stepped forward, and picked it up.

The puppy froze. Then it licked my chin.

The hall exploded again.

But I did not care.

Because the moment my fingers touched its head, a notification rang inside my mind.

**Hidden bond detected.**

**Lifebound contract available.**

**Warning: contract will bind soul, body, and growth path. Pain threshold extreme.**

**Proceed?**

I had sold almost everything I owned before coming here: training weights, spare gear, old books, even the little bracelet my mother had left behind. My little sister had cried when she saw the empty shelf in our room, but she still pushed her last saved coin into my palm and told me to come back with a future.

Every coin had gone toward one thing: beast meat.

Not for myself.

For this puppy.

Everyone else saw failure.

I saw the faint black mark under its fur, the one described only once in an old forbidden bestiary.

Devouring bloodline.

Limitless appetite.

Limitless growth.

I chose yes.

Pain tore through me so violently my knees hit the floor. It felt as if invisible claws had hooked into my soul and dragged it through fire. The puppy whimpered and pressed its forehead against mine. Around us, the laughter turned sharper.

"He collapsed."

"He really broke."

"All that pride, gone because of one useless dog."

Footsteps stopped in front of me.

Lu Ru Yan stood there in mage robes, beautiful enough that half the school still held its breath whenever she passed. Her expression carried practiced regret.

"Chenlin," she said, "I'm sorry. The entrance exam is the only ladder people like us have. Luang invited me to his team. I can't afford to fall with you."

I forced myself upright. My bones still ached from the contract, but my mind had become strangely clear.

"No problem," I said.

That was all.

Her face stiffened. Maybe she had expected anger. Maybe pleading. Maybe a question that would let her explain how hard the choice had been.

I gave her none of it.

She turned away with a cold little sound. Beside her, Ya Chowo sneered.

"Still pretending to be calm? You summoned a palm-sized mutt. What academy would take you now?"

I looked down at the puppy in my arms.

Another notification unfolded.

**Lifebound connection complete.**

**Summoned Beast: Hey Ming**

**Talent awakened: Gluttonous Greed**

**Effect: converts consumed matter into growth energy. Evolution shackles removed. Partial power feedback granted to contractor.**

My heartbeat thundered once.

Then again.

I lifted the puppy to eye level.

"From today on, your name is Hey Ming," I whispered. "Eat well. Grow fast. We have a lot of people to disappoint."

Hey Ming barked.

It sounded ridiculous.

I smiled anyway.

That night, while the rest of the students celebrated their teams, I carried Hey Ming to the cheapest butcher that sold monster-beast meat. The owner looked at my school badge, then at the little dog in my arms, and shook his head.

"Kid, that thing won't survive mutant meat."

"Pack all of it," I said.

"All?"

"Every pound I can afford."

By the time I returned to my rented room, the meat filled half the floor. Hey Ming stared at it. Its small body trembled, not with fear, but hunger so deep it seemed older than instinct.

"Go on," I said.

The puppy moved.

At first, it ate like any starving animal. Then black light flickered between its teeth. The first slab of meat vanished. Then the second. Then the third. Energy rolled through the room like heat from a furnace.

Hey Ming's legs lengthened. Its shoulders widened. Its fur deepened from dull black to a darkness that swallowed lamplight. With every bite, a thread of power flowed back through our bond and into my body.

My fingers clenched.

Strength.

Real strength.

Not borrowed reputation. Not a teacher's hope. Not a girlfriend's pity.

Mine.

Half an hour later, the palm-sized puppy was gone.

In its place stood a black wolf taller than my waist, eyes bright with intelligence, claws scraping grooves into the floorboards.

My own reflection in the cracked window startled me.

I looked the same at first glance: same worn training jacket, same tired eyes, same bloodless face from enduring the contract. But when I raised my hand, the movement felt too precise. The little tremor that had always come after long training sessions was gone. My breathing deepened on its own, and each breath carried heat through my ribs.

I picked up the dull iron practice knife from my desk.

Before the ceremony, it had felt heavy after a hundred swings.

Now it felt like a toy.

I moved once.

The blade cut through the air with a whistle so sharp Hey Ming's ears perked up. I stopped immediately, staring at the knife in my hand.

This was the feedback.

Not a blessing. Not a buff with a timer.

A permanent return on everything Hey Ming consumed.

The room around me suddenly felt too small. The floorboards, the thin walls, the ceiling low enough to trap heat, the old bedding folded in the corner. For years, this room had been proof of where I came from. Tonight, for the first time, it felt like something I would outgrow.

I sat down beside Hey Ming and pressed my forehead against his.

"Do not grow arrogant," I whispered, though I was not sure whether I meant him or myself.

Hey Ming yawned, completely unconcerned with philosophy.

The system chimed again.

**Evolution value threshold reached.**

**Begin first evolution?**

I did not hesitate.

Black flame erupted from Hey Ming's body. The room shook. Bones cracked, muscles rewove, and the wolf's shadow expanded against the walls until it looked less like an animal and more like a beast born from the end of a battlefield.

When the flames sank back into its fur, Hey Ming opened its eyes.

**Skill acquired: Rend.**

**Talent acquired: Gigantification.**

I stood in the silence, feeling the power still moving through me.

Tomorrow was the entrance exam.

The school expected me to fail.

Lu Ru Yan expected me to disappear.

Teacher Luen expected a tragedy.

I placed my hand on Hey Ming's head.

"Sleep," I said. "Tomorrow, we start hunting."

## Burial Bone Forest

The entrance exam plaza was already packed when I arrived.

Four bronze dungeon gates stood at the far end of the square, each one tall enough to swallow a building. Teachers moved between student teams, checking wrist recorders and giving last warnings. Parents and academy observers filled the viewing galleries above, their faces bright with expectation.

Lu Ru Yan stood beside Luang and Ya Chowo. When she saw me alone, something complicated moved through her eyes.

Pity, maybe.

Or relief.

I looked away first.

Teacher Luen approached with a deep frown. "Chenlin, where is your team?"

"I am entering alone."

His expression changed. "This is not a place for stubbornness."

"I know."

"Then why?"

I lifted my wrist and activated the recorder. "Because fewer teammates means a higher score multiplier."

For a moment, he seemed too stunned to speak.

The bell rang before he could stop me.

White light swallowed the plaza.

When my vision cleared, I stood beneath the twisted canopy of Burial Bone Forest. The air smelled of moss, rot, and old bones. Branches interlocked overhead, turning daylight into green shadow.

A soft click came from above.

I moved before I thought.

A dark serpent dropped from the branches, its mouth splitting open to reveal needles of venom. My staff appeared in my hand and shifted into a blade. One slash. One white arc. The serpent fell in two pieces.

**Venom Fang Worm slain.**

**Score +15.**

Outside, the viewing gallery went silent.

I could imagine their faces.

A summoner was supposed to stand behind his beast.

That was the first rule every instructor taught.

The second rule was never let emotion break formation.

The third was never chase points alone.

I had already broken all three.

But rules were built for people walking ordinary roads. They assumed a summoner's body remained weak. They assumed a beast needed commands, positioning, and recovery time. They assumed growth came slowly, through carefully measured training cycles and academy-issued resources.

Hey Ming had eaten those assumptions for dinner.

I walked forward.

The forest answered with shrieks.

Small green figures poured from the undergrowth. Thirty. Forty. Then more. Goblins with bone knives, crude spears, and mouths full of broken teeth.

I raised one hand.

"Hey Ming."

Black light tore open beside me.

The wolf landed in the middle of the swarm.

The first goblin did not even have time to scream. Hey Ming's shoulder struck it like a falling wall, crushing bone and armor together. Spears snapped against his back. Knives skidded off his fur. Then his claws came down.

The pack broke in less than a minute.

I moved with him, sword in hand, cutting through every gap he opened. Goblins fled. I let a few live long enough to run.

They led us to a mountain cave.

Inside, the nest stank of smoke and blood. Goblins clung to the walls, dropped from ledges, and surged through tunnels with torches raised. Their numbers would have swallowed an ordinary team.

The first wave came from the ground.

The second came from the walls.

The third came from above, dropping nets woven from sinew and bone hooks. I cut through one net before it touched me. Hey Ming tore through two more by simply expanding his shoulders, the hooks snapping against his fur.

Then the flame goblins appeared deeper in the cavern.

Their chants bounced strangely off the stone, making it hard to tell where the spells would form. Fireballs gathered in the air like red fruit. Frost goblins answered from the other side, their breath turning the cave mist white.

I grabbed a charging goblin by the throat and threw it directly into the first fireball.

The explosion lit the cavern.

For one heartbeat, every goblin stopped.

Outside the dungeon, I knew some instructor had just realized I was using the enemy's own formation against them.

I threw the next goblin harder.

This time, three fireballs detonated at once.

Hey Ming did not waste the opening. He passed through the confusion like a black blade, jaws closing, claws falling, bodies vanishing into flame.

Hey Ming grew.

Gigantification spread through him in a wave of black force. His spine rose, his paws widened, and the cave trembled beneath his weight.

Then the troll appeared.

It was a hulking thing with arms like tree trunks and eyes full of dull violence. Its roar sent dust falling from the cavern roof. Hey Ming met it head-on.

The impact shook the cave.

I kept enhancement arts active with one hand and recovery arts with the other. Hey Ming tore flesh. The troll crushed stone. Nearby students arrived at the entrance, then stopped dead when they saw there was no team fighting the boss.

Only me.

Only Hey Ming.

When the troll's health dropped to its final thread, I drove my sword through its throat.

**Boss slain.**

**Score +1200.**

The dungeon trembled.

Outside, Second High erupted.

Teacher Luen stared at the water screen as if it had personally betrayed his understanding of reality. Li Mingji, the school leader, leaned forward with both hands on the railing.

"That is Chenlin?" he asked.

Luen swallowed. "Yes."

"The boy with the puppy?"

"Yes."

On another screen, Su Ling Shu stood alone in a frost-covered grove, her sword dripping monster blood. She looked up at the ranking panel.

First place: Chenlin.

For the first time that day, her expression changed.

## The Recruiters

By sunset, my name had crossed the city.

By midnight, academy representatives were waiting at Second High.

Ji Fei from Yunk Academy arrived first. She wore a relaxed smile and carried herself like a person who had never needed to raise her voice to win a room.

Fang Yi from Chintian Academy arrived ten minutes later with a resource contract thick enough to make Teacher Luen's eyes twitch. His younger sister, Fang Ya, came with him, a quiet girl with sharp eyes who watched me as if she were already trying to solve the puzzle of Hey Ming's growth.

Then came the others.

They argued politely for exactly three minutes before the politeness cracked.

"Chenlin belongs at Yunk Academy," Ji Fei said. "We train irregular talents. We do not bury them under standard methods."

Fang Yi laughed. "You mean you gamble on unstable monsters and hope the academy survives."

"Better than buying students and pretending money is guidance."

Fang Ya looked at Fang Yi and said softly, "Brother, if you keep insulting him, he will choose her."

Fang Yi's jaw tightened.

I listened until the room quieted.

"I choose Yunk Academy."

Fang Yi's smile vanished.

Ji Fei's widened.

"Good," she said. "Pack lightly. Your old life is over."

She was right, but not in the way she meant.

At Yunk Academy, the classrooms were clean, the training grounds enormous, and the students strong enough that many had been treated as miracles in their home provinces.

Xiao Huiyang entered first among the academy's famous rookies, smiling with the easy confidence of someone raised around power. Behind him came Xiao Yang, younger and more restless, and Xiao Yun Shu, whose calm gaze made even the noisiest students lower their voices. Lu Ning sat near the aisle with a stack of tactical notes already open. Lu Xiaoyu claimed the back window and watched every entrance like a scout. Wang Yao looked at the training field more than the instructor. Meow Shan Shan, small and bright-eyed, had a summoned beast charm swinging from her wrist. Lia Curly arrived late with healer badges pinned crookedly to her uniform and acted as if that was the room's problem.

Luolan and Luolu, twin support students from a border city, sat together and spoke in low voices. They looked harmless until I noticed both had military-grade wrist recorders.

The classroom measured me the moment I entered.

That was normal. Every elite student had learned to rank strangers by posture, equipment, breathing rhythm, and the invisible pressure of class energy. In their home cities, each of them had probably been the brightest name on the local board. Here, they all had to discover how small a local miracle could feel.

Xiao Huiyang smiled first. "Chenlin, right? The summoner who broke the entrance exam ranking."

"I passed it," I said.

"You humiliated it."

Luwan leaned over from the next desk. "He says that like it is a criticism, but his eyes are sparkling. Be careful. Rich young masters collect interesting people the way normal people collect shoes."

Xiao Huiyang laughed instead of taking offense. "Interesting people survive longer."

Su Ling Shu looked up from her seat near the window. "Not always."

The room quieted.

Her voice was calm, but it carried the cold edge of someone who had seen talent die before. For a moment, no one treated the academy like a prize.

Yet on the first day, Ji Fei stood before us and told the truth no broadcast had dared to say.

The alien clans were preparing for war.

The mutant beast fronts were worsening.

Humanity had not been winning.

It had been buying time.

I looked around the classroom. Some students went pale. Others clenched their fists. Su Ling Shu sat near the window, expression unreadable. Lu Hongying leaned back as if this was exactly the kind of trouble she had expected. Lu Ning began rewriting her notes before Ji Fei finished speaking. Lu Xiaoyu's eyes sharpened. Lia Curly quietly checked the emergency healing kit at her waist.

Luwan looked at me and whispered, "Brother Lin, did we join an academy or a battlefield?"

"Both," I said.

Ji Fei heard me and smiled.

"Correct," she said. "The academy is where you learn enough to avoid dying uselessly. The battlefield is where we learn whether that lesson took."

She tapped the holographic wall.

The map changed.

Red marks appeared beyond the border zones, more than any public report had ever shown. Some pulsed steadily. Others flickered, meaning contact had been lost. Around those marks were smaller blue points: bases, outposts, evacuation cities, and old fortresses still holding by habit more than strength.

"The media calls this stability," Ji Fei said. "The military calls it containment. The difference is that containment has a price, and that price is paid by people whose names you rarely hear."

No one spoke.

"You are not here because you are special," she continued. "You are here because the country has spent resources betting that you might become useful before the wall breaks."

That sentence landed harder than praise would have.

Useful.

Not glorious.

Not chosen.

Useful.

I felt Hey Ming stir in the summoning space, sensing the shift in my mood.

I did not calm him.

Some truths deserved teeth.

## Silver Butterfly

My second summon should have been impossible.

The academy gave me rare materials, a protected summoning room, and enough energy crystals to support a high-tier beast birth. The circle burned for nearly five minutes.

When the light cleared, a silver butterfly drifted into the air.

It was beautiful.

It was also weaker than Hey Ming had been as a puppy.

The stat panel was almost insulting.

**Strength: 1**

**Constitution: 1**

**Agility: 1**

**Magic: 1**

Luwan, watching from the observation window, covered his face. "Again?"

I held out my finger.

The butterfly landed there, wings shining with scarlet lines.

"Do not worry," I said. "Weak beginnings are familiar to us."

Its talent appeared when the first rare metal touched its wings.

Silver light flashed.

The metal dissolved.

Then armor formed over my arm.

Not summoned armor. Not equipment.

A living weapon.

Silver Butterfly could fuse materials into itself, inherit their properties, and reshape as armor, blade, shield, or wing. Every rare metal became part of a growing arsenal.

Ji Fei stared through the glass.

"Chenlin," she said slowly, "do you only summon things that rewrite textbooks?"

"So far."

The academy's resource department never forgave me.

Within three days, Silver Butterfly had devoured enough rare metals to become a full armor system. When it wrapped around me, my body felt both heavier and faster, as if the world had slowed down around the edge of my blade.

That was when the missions began.

## Disaster Trial

The transcendence trial opened beneath my feet and dropped me into a dying city.

The sky was cracked red. Buildings leaned like broken teeth. In the streets, skinless disaster beasts crawled over abandoned vehicles and through shattered shopfronts.

A mission panel appeared.

**Option One: eliminate disaster beasts and preserve survivors.**

**Option Two: assist the Disaster Matriarch in devouring the world.**

I laughed.

"Assist her in devouring everything?"

Hey Ming emerged beside me, black flames leaking from his jaws.

"She can wait her turn."

I chose salvation.

The trial was supposed to test judgment. For the other participants, it became a hunt for points. Some saved civilians. Some hid. Some chose the Matriarch, believing destruction would bring better rewards.

I found the first survivors beneath a collapsed subway entrance.

There were eleven of them: two old men, a wounded woman, three children, and several exhausted guards whose rifles had long since run dry. A disaster beast scratched at the metal door above them, peeling strips from it with claws like rusted hooks.

When I cut the beast in half, the people below did not cheer.

They flinched.

In this world, rescue had become another shape danger could wear.

I crouched at the edge of the stairs and held up my empty hand. "I am not with the Matriarch."

One guard laughed bitterly. "Everyone says that before they ask where the healthy children are."

For a moment, I had no answer.

Then Diana's catalyst, still dormant in my storage space, warmed faintly, as if reacting to the misery around me.

I took out food, water, and a spare recorder beacon.

"There is a shelter route three streets east," I said. "Wait for the next quiet gap, then move. If anything follows you, throw this beacon."

The wounded woman stared at the supplies. "Why help us? The trial rewards kills."

"It also rewards choices."

I left before they could thank me.

Gratitude would have slowed them down.

Those people found me first.

Seven enemies blocked the street: a golden-haired lightning caster, a delicate assassin woman, a hulking flame man, and four others with murder already in their eyes.

"Chenlin," the lightning caster said, "your score ends here."

Hey Ming's shadow stretched behind them.

"No," I said. "Yours does."

The battle lasted less than a minute.

The flame man froze beneath an ally's ice burst. The assassin tried to vanish and was dragged screaming into black fire. The lightning caster ran after losing an arm.

I caught him at the end of the street.

"Tell me about the Disaster Matriarch."

He spat blood and smiled. "She is the only future this world has left."

"Wrong."

The Matriarch's nest pulsed at the city's center, a tower wrapped in red veins and bone-white tendrils. When she emerged, she was not merely a monster. She was hunger with a mind, a creature born from a world that had forgotten how to live.

Hey Ming stepped forward.

For the first time, another devourer looked afraid of him.

Their clash split the tower open. The Matriarch's tendrils swallowed rubble, corpses, and living beasts. Hey Ming's black flame swallowed her tendrils in return.

I cut through the core while Silver Butterfly shielded me from corruption.

When the Matriarch fell, the red sky faded.

Survivors crawled from the ruins and stared at the black wolf standing over the dead world-eater.

**Trial complete.**

**Maximum evaluation achieved.**

**Reward: sacred summoning catalyst.**

At the time, I did not know what that meant.

I learned the next week.

## Diana

The summoning room filled with gold light.

Not white.

Not blue.

Gold.

Every instructor outside the chamber straightened. Ji Fei's lazy expression disappeared. Even Yang Xiao, the academy protector assigned to observe me after the trial, narrowed his eyes.

Something small fell from the light.

I caught her.

She was a tiny angel with pale hair, trembling wings, and corruption marks fading from her skin. Her hands gripped my sleeve.

"Master?" she whispered.

The room went silent.

Angels did not answer human summons. They especially did not call anyone master.

"Your name?" I asked softly.

She blinked as if remembering hurt. "Diana."

Later, in my dormitory, she told me what little she could.

Her sacred tree had been attacked. Her people had fallen. Black corruption had dragged her from the branches, and my summon had pulled her away before it finished eating her soul.

I touched her hair with one finger.

"Then grow here," I said. "When you are strong enough, we will find the ones who did it."

Diana nodded seriously, though her wings still shook.

Hey Ming lowered his head to sniff her.

She hugged his nose.

The devouring wolf froze, utterly defeated.

From that day, our team changed.

Hey Ming was destruction.

Silver Butterfly was weapon and armor.

Diana was blessing, purification, and judgment waiting to mature.

And I was the thread holding them together.

## The Mountain Missions

The military sent us to Hangdon Base to clear mutant beasts above our official rank.

The officers thought I was reckless.

They were correct.

Our temporary field squad included Ji Kong, Yi Kexi, Lu Ning, Lu Xiaoyu, Lia Curly, and Wang Yao. Ji Kong wanted to test himself against everything with teeth. Yi Kexi said she preferred tests that did not bite back. Lu Ning mapped threat routes. Lu Xiaoyu ranged ahead and marked ambush points. Lia Curly kept everyone alive with a patience that grew thinner each hour. Wang Yao carried a heavy blade and spoke only when he thought someone was about to do something stupid.

The first target was a Berserk Black Ape, a level 33 beast with arms thick enough to crush armored vehicles. I killed it without calling Hey Ming, using Silver Butterfly's blade form and the speed fed back through our bond.

The officers watching through drone feed nearly ordered a rescue anyway.

I heard the helicopter rotors before I saw the ape. Four armed aircraft kept distance above the canopy, trying to pretend they were not hovering over my head like anxious parents. Lu Ning's voice came through the squad channel, flat and professional.

"Target moving northeast. It has noticed you."

"Good."

"That was not a recommendation."

The Berserk Black Ape burst through the trees with both fists raised. Its roar shook leaves loose for fifty meters around it. I let it close the distance, waited until its shadow covered me, then stepped inside the arc of its punch.

Silver Butterfly became a blade along my forearm.

One cut opened the tendon behind its wrist.

The ape's fist smashed into the ground beside me instead of through my chest. It tried to bite. I pivoted, climbed its arm in three steps, and drove the blade under its jaw.

When it fell, the entire forest seemed to jump.

For several seconds, no one spoke on the channel.

Then Luwan, who was not supposed to be on that frequency, whispered, "I am changing my career goal from hero to Chenlin's background decoration."

The second was a Twin Fang Giant Tiger. It nearly took my left arm. Silver Butterfly shifted into a shield at the last instant, then a spear, then a winged blade that opened the tiger's throat.

The third was a Giant Rock Drake.

That fight nearly went wrong.

The drake's gravity field crushed the canyon floor into powder and pinned Ji Kong to one knee before he could raise his shield. Yi Kexi poured buffs into Hey Ming until sweat ran down her face. Lia Curly kept a healing barrier around Lu Xiaoyu, who had been thrown into a cliff wall while marking the drake's blind side.

"Retreat one kilometer," I ordered.

Wang Yao looked at the drake, then at me. "That is not a plan. That is a warning label."

"Move."

They moved.

Hey Ming's black flame phantom rose behind him, vast and starving. The Giant Rock Drake's stone scales melted layer by layer, and the gravity field collapsed beneath a pressure far more ancient than weight.

The fourth was a Blood Spirit Colossal Crocodile.

That one required Hey Ming.

The crocodile rose from a red marsh like a living island. Hey Ming met it in black fire, tore out its spine, and devoured its life crystal before the officers watching by drone remembered to breathe.

Yang Xiao called someone immediately after.

"How strong is he?" the voice asked.

Yang Xiao looked at the feed, where Hey Ming was eating a beast that should have required an elite squad.

"Strong enough that if the alien clans are smart, they will try to kill him before he grows."

The voice went quiet.

Then it said, "Then we make sure they fail."

They did try.

Fallen Clan assassins came through a border ruin under cover of a Shadow Congregation ritual. They brought corruption blades, curse arrays, and enough sacrificial energy to kill a city block.

Their leaders were Leakru and Leardis, twin Fallen Clan commanders wearing bone-white masks. Leakru carried a spear that bled smoke. Leardis controlled shadow chains that could drag a soul half out of its body. Behind them, Jiang Wu and Joe Yuan, two human officers stationed at the border, fought desperately to hold the breach long enough for students to withdraw.

Diana raised one hand.

Golden light burned the curse array away.

Silver Butterfly wrapped me in armor.

Hey Ming laughed through his teeth.

By dawn, the ruins were silent.

Jiang Wu survived with a scar across his chest. Joe Yuan lost half his armor but refused evacuation until he personally counted every student. Leakru fell beneath Hey Ming's claws. Leardis tried to flee through his own ritual shadow, only for Silver Butterfly to cut the darkness open and leave him nowhere to hide.

Humanity's leaders stopped calling me a genius.

They started calling me an asset.

I hated that word.

But I understood the fear behind it.

## Balian Mountain Range

The Balian Mountain Range was supposed to take months to stabilize.

We finished it in days.

Hey Ming hunted without rest. Level 50 beasts, level 60 beasts, dark bears, horned bulls, twisted tree kings, and things old enough to have names in military archives all vanished into black flame.

The Raging Horned Bull charged through three valleys before Hey Ming caught it by the horns and snapped its neck against a mountain wall. The Undying Berserk Bear rose seven times after being torn apart, and seven times Hey Ming devoured more of the strange vitality keeping it alive. The Three-Headed Serpent poisoned an entire lake, forcing Diana to purify the water while I cut away its left and right heads so Hey Ming could swallow the core.

Every time Hey Ming devoured a colossal beast, he grew.

Every time he grew, my own body changed.

My bones became harder. My senses sharpened. My magic reserves widened until ordinary summoners felt like candles beside a furnace.

When the final dark gold berserk bear tried to flee into the inner mountains, Hey Ming chased it across three ridges and dragged it back by the neck.

At the command center, officers cheered.

Yang Xiao did not.

He watched the empty beast territories on the map and frowned.

"They are gathering elsewhere," he said.

He was right.

The first clue was silence.

Not peace.

Silence.

Drone feeds from the outer perimeter showed forests without movement. No scavengers. No small beasts fighting over territory. No level 20 packs rushing in to claim the spaces left by dead kings.

Every creature with enough instinct to fear a command had vanished.

Commander reports came in one after another. Empty valleys. Abandoned nests. Tracks all pointed west.

Yang Xiao replayed the footage three times, then zoomed in on a broken ridge where hundreds of claw marks overlapped.

"This is not migration," he said. "This is mustering."

Ji Fei folded her arms. "For Wooan?"

"For Wooan, Yuneh, and every civilian road between them."

The room lost its celebration in a breath.

Only then did I understand why Yang Xiao had not smiled when the Balian map turned blue. We had not ended the danger. We had pushed the surviving command structure to reveal its hand.

Sometimes victory was only a louder alarm.

Wooan Base sent the emergency call that night.

Four beast tides.

Multiple colossal leaders.

One level 69 Dark Gold Berserk Bear king nearing the next breakthrough.

Wooan City had tens of millions still evacuating.

If the base fell, the city would become a feeding ground.

I boarded the transport aircraft before Yang Xiao finished asking.

Diana sat on my shoulder, tiny hands gripping my collar.

Luwan leaned across the aisle. Luolan and Luolu sat behind him, both checking defensive talismans with identical frowns. Xiao Yang had smuggled extra ammunition into his pack, while Xiao Yun Shu quietly pretended not to notice. Xiao Huiyang smiled at everyone as if this were a school trip and not a probable disaster.

"Brother Lin," Luwan said, "you know normal people hesitate before going to certain death."

"Good thing I brought abnormal company."

Hey Ming's summoning mark pulsed under my sleeve, offended that he had not been allowed inside the aircraft.

## Wooan

Commander Cao Wu met us on the airstrip with dust on his uniform and exhaustion in his eyes.

"Cadet Chenlin," he said, gripping my hand with both of his, "I have heard the reports."

"Then you know I need materials."

For the first time in days, he smiled.

"Already prepared."

The logistics warehouse held crates of rare metals and life crystals. Silver Butterfly rose from my palm and unfolded its wings.

The metals melted into silver light.

Armor formed.

Heavier.

Sharper.

Alive.

The warehouse floor dented beneath my first step.

**Silver Butterfly property acquired: Lava Edge.**

**Property stack enhanced: Sharpness.**

**Property acquired: Scale At Will.**

I flexed my fingers and watched silver plates shift like liquid.

"Now," I said, "show me the battlefield."

Commander Cao Wu gathered the cadets and officers in an open briefing yard. Holographic maps lit the air. Beast tides advanced from four directions like living storms.

"Your task," he said, "is to intercept stragglers, reduce numbers, and avoid direct contact with colossal leaders unless ordered."

Yang Xiao glanced at me.

I glanced back.

He sighed. "Chenlin will operate alone."

Several cadets looked at me as if I had just been sentenced.

Xiao Huiyang raised his hand. "Commander, assign the Xiao squad to civilian evacuation. Xiao Yang and Xiao Yun Shu can handle route clearing."

Commander Cao Wu nodded. "Approved."

Luolan and Luolu were assigned to shield relay duty. Lu Ning took tactical coordination. Lu Xiaoyu disappeared into the scout line before anyone finished giving her permission. Meow Shan Shan joined the support group with Lia Curly, carrying medical charms and pretending she was not terrified.

I summoned Hey Ming.

The air cracked.

The black wolf appeared at over a hundred meters tall, then lowered himself so I could step onto his back. Diana climbed into my arms, eyes bright with battlefield curiosity.

One cadet whispered, "That is not a summoned beast. That is a natural disaster with fur."

Hey Ming heard him and looked pleased.

We departed before anyone could revise the mission.

The beast tide stretched across the mountain plain for kilometers. Thousands of mutant beasts moved together, crushing trees, stones, and old roads beneath their feet. Above them walked three colossal beasts, each large enough to cast shadows over valleys.

From the ground, a beast tide did not look like an army.

It looked like geography had decided to move.

The front ranks were made of smaller beasts, each one still large enough to overturn a vehicle. Behind them came armored quadrupeds with bone plates layered over their backs. Flying beasts circled overhead in ragged spirals. Farther back, enormous shadows pushed through the dust: the colossal leaders, slow only because nothing in front of them could survive long enough to make them hurry.

Diana held my collar tighter.

"There are so many," she whispered.

"Yes."

"Are all of them enemies?"

I looked at the tide. Some were mad with hunger. Some were bound by instinct. Some were probably only running because the stronger beasts behind them left no room to refuse.

"Today," I said, "they are."

She went quiet.

That was one of the crueler lessons of war. Not every enemy was evil. Some were simply moving in the wrong direction at the wrong time, toward people who had no room left to retreat.

The mission had been to cut down stragglers.

I pointed at the center.

"There."

Hey Ming charged.

The sound barrier shattered around us. Dust exploded outward. Beasts near the impact point died before his claws reached them, crushed by force alone.

Commander Cao Wu's voice burst through the wristband. "Chenlin! That is the main formation!"

"I noticed."

Hey Ming's black flame domain unfolded.

The world went dark.

Every beast below a certain level began to burn from the inside. Life crystals cracked. Flesh became ash. The three colossal leaders roared and charged, finally understanding that they were not facing prey.

The first colossal beast lowered its head and charged like a mountain breaking loose.

The second opened its mouth and released a stream of ghost-blue fire that turned stone glassy wherever it passed.

The third vanished into the dust, burrowing under the battlefield with speed that should have been impossible for something so large.

I sent the battlefield image through the wristband.

"Commander Cao Wu, mark the burrower."

"We cannot track it under that much interference."

"Then track me."

Silver Butterfly unfolded a sensor wing from my shoulder. The ground beneath Hey Ming trembled. I felt the burrower approaching through the bond a heartbeat before it emerged.

"Left."

Hey Ming jumped.

The burrowing colossal beast erupted where his body had been, jaws closing on empty air. Hey Ming landed on its neck with enough force to flatten a hill, then drove it back into the earth.

The command channel went completely silent.

Then Commander Cao Wu said, very carefully, "Cadet Chenlin, are you using yourself as bait for a colossal beast?"

"Only briefly."

Diana flew upward.

"Master," she said, her small voice suddenly calm, "I can fight too."

Gold light broke around her.

Her child form stretched into an adult silhouette, beautiful and cold beneath a rising moon. Nine ring blades formed around her, each one wrapped in dark red force.

The moon behind her turned crimson.

The battlefield froze.

Diana pointed.

The first ring blade cut open a colossal beast's shoulder.

The second and third tore through its armor.

Hey Ming met the other two head-on, his giant phantom rising behind him like an executioner made of night. Claws fell. Mountains cracked. The beast tide scattered in panic, trampling itself to escape.

Then Diana fused the nine blades into one.

The red ring grew until it seemed to divide the sky.

"Welcome your deaths," she said.

The blade fell.

One colossal beast died in a single line of crimson light.

Another split in two before it could flee.

Diana's moon shattered. Her adult form collapsed back into the small angel I knew, and she fluttered down unsteadily.

I caught her.

"Too much?" I asked.

She nodded, then tried to look dignified while half-asleep against my chest.

Hey Ming finished the final beast by pinning it beneath one paw and tearing out its core.

The tide broke.

Wooan's western front was clear.

Inside the command center, no one spoke for a long time.

Commander Cao Wu finally sat down.

"The mission," he said faintly, "was to intercept stragglers."

Yang Xiao covered his face.

"He has trouble with small instructions."

## Dark Gold Berserk Bear King

The victory should have saved Wooan.

Instead, it woke the real commander.

The level 69 Dark Gold Berserk Bear king had been hiding beyond the sensor range, waiting for the four tides to exhaust the base. When one front vanished, it abandoned caution.

Night fell early.

A dark-gold mane rose over the mountains like a burning ridge.

The Dark Gold Berserk Bear King stepped into view.

It was larger than every beast we had fought that day. Its hide drank light. Its eyes carried cruel intelligence. Around it gathered the remaining tides, drawn by fear and command.

Commander Cao Wu ordered all artillery to fire.

The shells struck.

Smoke covered the mountain.

The Dark Gold Berserk Bear King walked through it unharmed.

Yang Xiao took one step forward, then coughed blood. The old curse in his body flared, black veins crawling up his neck.

Diana lifted her hand, but her sacred power had not recovered.

I looked at Hey Ming.

He looked back.

Neither of us needed words.

"Silver Butterfly."

Armor flowed over me, layer after layer, until my body felt like a blade wearing human shape.

"Diana, bless Hey Ming with whatever you have left."

She pressed both hands to Hey Ming's forehead. A small gold mark flickered there.

Tiny.

Fragile.

Enough.

Hey Ming grew.

Not to a hundred meters.

Not to a thousand.

He grew until the battlefield itself seemed to bend around him, black flames spiraling up into the clouds. Behind him, the brutal phantom opened its gray eyes.

The Dark Gold Berserk Bear King spoke, its voice rolling over the mountains.

"Devourer. Kneel, and I will let you live as my hound."

Hey Ming's answer was to bite through the nearest ridge and spit molten stone at its face.

I laughed despite myself.

Then the final battle began.

The Dark Gold Berserk Bear King was not merely large. It controlled razor-edged dark-gold force like a weapon. Crescent shock waves carved through the ground. Black-gold chains rose from the earth. Entire squads would have died in the first exchange.

Hey Ming tore through them.

Silver Butterfly carried me along the broken terrain, shifting from armor to wings to blade as I cut apart the smaller beasts trying to interfere. Every life crystal I seized went straight into Hey Ming's flame.

More power.

More hunger.

But the Bear King learned.

The first time Hey Ming lunged, it met him head-on.

The second time, it stepped aside and let Hey Ming's own momentum carry him into a field of black-gold spikes. The spikes shattered against his shoulder, but the impact opened a line of blood beneath the fur.

The third time, the Bear King ignored Hey Ming completely and struck at me.

Its claw crossed hundreds of meters in a blur. Silver Butterfly reacted faster than my thoughts, forming five overlapping shields. Four broke instantly. The fifth held long enough for Diana's faint gold barrier to bloom across it.

The shock still threw me through the air.

For two seconds, the world became sky, dust, and pain.

I hit the ground hard enough to carve a trench through broken stone. My ribs protested. My ears rang. Somewhere far away, Luwan screamed my name over the channel.

I forced myself up.

The Bear King was watching me.

Not Hey Ming.

Me.

It had understood the contract.

Kill the summoner, and the beast would lose its anchor.

Hey Ming's fury hit the bond like a storm.

"No," I said through blood in my mouth. "Do not lose yourself."

The order was not for obedience.

It was for trust.

The Dark Gold Berserk Bear King drove one massive claw through Hey Ming's shoulder.

For the first time, Hey Ming bled heavily.

Pain tore through our bond and nearly dropped me to one knee.

Then another notification rang.

**Lifebound resonance threshold reached.**

**Contractor and summoned beast may initiate shared evolution.**

**Warning: failure will damage both souls.**

I did not hesitate.

"Proceed."

Hey Ming howled.

I screamed with him.

Our shadows merged.

Black flame entered my armor. Silver Butterfly adapted instantly, turning the devouring fire into engraved lines across my chest, arms, and blade. Diana's gold seal flared at the center of it all, stopping the flame from consuming me.

For one moment, I saw through Hey Ming's eyes.

The world was food.

Power was food.

Fear was food.

Fate itself was only something that had not yet been bitten hard enough.

I raised my sword.

Hey Ming raised his claws.

We struck together.

The Dark Gold Berserk Bear King's foreclaw shattered.

Its darkness collapsed.

Hey Ming's jaws closed around its throat while my blade pierced its core.

The king thrashed, splitting the mountain beneath us. Then black flame poured into its body and devoured everything: flesh, shadow, command authority, and the half-formed breakthrough power that had made it so dangerous.

When it died, every beast tide lost direction at once.

Wooan Base opened fire.

The remaining monsters scattered.

Dawn rose over a battlefield of ash.

## The Alien Gate

The world celebrated Wooan for exactly two days before the alien clans made their move.

They had not tried to kill me at Wooan because they were afraid.

They had tried to measure me.

Now they opened a gate.

It appeared above the old border defense zone, a wound in the sky rimmed with violet fire. Fallen Clan warriors descended first, then armored beasts, then a throne-level Fallen Clan warlord with four arms and a crown of bone.

Two Fallen Sky Phantoms drifted through the wound after him, thin and enormous, their bodies like torn cloaks stitched from night. Wherever they passed, soldiers forgot how to breathe and spells lost their shape.

Humanity's strongest gathered to stop it.

Yang Xiao came despite his curse.

Jiang Ju came wrapped in corruption wounds he pretended did not hurt.

Commander Cao Wu sent every weapon he could spare.

Su Ling Shu arrived with frost blooming beneath her feet. Lu Hongying, Luwan, Ji Kong, Yi Kexi, Wangfeng, and others came too, not because anyone ordered them, but because this was the kind of battle that decided whether orders would matter tomorrow.

Xiao Huiyang led the academy reserve line. Xiao Yang laughed too loudly while loading his weapon. Xiao Yun Shu stood beside him, silent and composed, her spellwork already forming a pale barrier across the ground. Lu Ning coordinated the left flank with frightening precision. Lu Xiaoyu marked alien scouts before they reached firing range. Wang Yao joined the vanguard. Meow Shan Shan and Lia Curly kept the wounded moving. Luolan and Luolu maintained the shield relays that stopped the first wave from breaking through.

Lu Ru Yan was there as well.

She found me before the gate opened fully.

"Chenlin," she said. "I was wrong."

The words were quiet.

Honest.

Too late to matter in the way she wanted.

I looked at the alien army forming beyond the gate.

"Then survive," I said. "That will be enough."

Her eyes lowered.

Then she nodded and joined the mage line.

It would have been easier if I hated her.

Hatred gave old pain a clean shape. It made every memory simple. It told you that the person who left had always been cruel, and the person who stayed standing had always been right.

But life had never been that generous.

Lu Ru Yan had been afraid. Ambitious. Practical. Wrong.

I had been proud. Silent. More willing to be misunderstood than to explain anything to someone who had already chosen to leave.

The world did not pause to judge either of us.

Above the border ruins, the gate widened.

Whatever we had been to each other belonged to a smaller life.

This one demanded survival first.

The Fallen Clan warlord descended.

Its pressure forced weaker soldiers to their knees.

"Humanity," it said, "your delay is over."

Jiang Ju wiped blood from his mouth. "I hate dramatic guests."

Yang Xiao drew his weapon. "You could have stayed home."

The Fallen Clan warlord smiled.

Then it saw Hey Ming.

For the first time, its smile faded.

"That thing should not exist."

I stepped onto Hey Ming's head, Silver Butterfly unfolding behind me like metal wings. Diana hovered at my side, gold light slowly turning silver. The sacred mark on her forehead opened like a tiny sun.

"People keep saying that," I replied.

The gate war began.

Humanity's lines clashed with the Fallen Clan. Su Ling Shu froze entire assault waves. Lu Hongying carved through elite enemies. Luwan somehow survived every impossible charge while shouting that he regretted being inspired by me.

The first Fallen Sky Phantom swept toward the healer line, and Meow Shan Shan screamed before throwing every charm she had into the air. Lia Curly turned the scattered charms into a healing barrier bright enough to keep the phantom back for three seconds.

Three seconds was enough.

Wang Yao's blade split the phantom's lower body. Xiao Yang's ammunition detonated inside the wound. Xiao Yun Shu's barrier folded inward like a closing book, pinning the phantom in place until Hey Ming's black flame reached it.

The second phantom tried to pass through Luolan and Luolu's shield relay. The twins linked hands, their talismans burning down to ash as they held the line. Lu Xiaoyu marked the phantom's core, and Su Ling Shu froze that mark into a spear of ice that pierced straight through it.

Everywhere I looked, someone who had once been only a name on a classroom seating chart was now holding part of the world together.

Ji Kong stood over a fallen squad and blocked three Fallen Clan warriors at once, shield cracked, teeth bared in something too stubborn to be called a smile. Yi Kexi kept a blessing chain connected to him even while blood ran from her nose.

Lu Ning's voice moved through the command channel like a blade through cloth.

"Left flank, step back two meters. Do not chase. Wangfeng, your civilian group has thirty seconds before breach. Xiao Huiyang, rotate reserve team now."

"Already moving," Xiao Huiyang answered.

Lu Hongying punched through an armored beast's chest and kicked the body into another enemy. "Someone tell Chenlin he is not allowed to die dramatically. I refuse to attend that funeral."

Luwan shouted from somewhere behind a broken wall, "Why are we discussing funerals while I am still using mine as motivation?"

For one impossible breath, I almost laughed.

Then the Fallen Clan warlord raised its hand, and the battlefield pressure doubled.

Above them, the throne-ranked fought.

Yang Xiao and Jiang Ju held the Fallen Clan warlord in place, but the corruption in their bodies worsened with every exchange. The warlord's bone crown radiated fallen power, the same power that had destroyed Diana's sacred tree.

Diana recognized it.

Her face changed.

"Master," she said, "that is the one."

I understood.

The general had not merely invaded Earth.

It had helped burn her home.

"Can you fight?"

Her small hands closed into fists.

"I can judge."

The sky changed.

The sun appeared behind Diana.

Then the moon.

Gold and crimson overlapped, forming a ring of impossible light. Her child form became adult again, but this time she did not look cold. She looked furious.

**Diana ultimate form awakened: Sun-Moon Judgment.**

Hey Ming's black flame rose to meet her light.

Silver Butterfly opened every blade it had.

The Fallen Clan warlord tried to retreat into the gate.

Too late.

Diana's sun burned the corruption from Yang Xiao and Jiang Ju, freeing them for one final attack. The moon pinned the Fallen Clan warlord's shadow to the sky. Hey Ming lunged upward, biting into the gate itself.

The world screamed.

Space cracked between his teeth.

"Impossible!" the general roared.

I drove Silver Butterfly's blade through its crown.

Diana's judgment fell.

The Fallen Clan warlord burned white, then black, then vanished.

Hey Ming devoured the gate.

Not closed it.

Devoured it.

Every alien still connected to the passage felt that hunger and fled screaming into their own realm.

The sky healed.

For a long time, no one moved.

Then Luwan raised one shaking hand.

"So," he said, "does this mean classes are canceled?"

Ji Fei, bloodied and smiling, slapped the back of his head.

## Sacred Tree

Months passed.

The alien clans did not launch another invasion. They tested the borders, sent scouts, and lost them. Humanity rebuilt the damaged bases and stopped pretending the war had been distant.

My rank rose.

Hey Ming grew.

Silver Butterfly became a named divine armament in all but official classification.

Diana, however, grew quiet.

Her power had awakened, but the memory of her fallen sacred tree remained like a thorn in the bond. So when Yang Xiao's curse was finally cleansed and the border stabilized, I asked him for coordinates.

"To where?" he asked.

"Diana's home."

He stared at me. "That is beyond the recovered gate path."

"I know."

"It may be dead."

Diana stood beside me, wings still.

"Then we should bury it," she said.

Yang Xiao looked at her for a long moment.

Then he gave us the coordinates.

The sacred realm was not dead.

But it was close.

The tree stood at the center of a floating island, its branches blackened, its leaves gone, its roots wrapped in fallen chains. Around it drifted the remains of angelic halls and broken golden bridges.

Diana walked forward without speaking.

No enemies came.

They had stripped the realm and abandoned it.

That somehow felt worse.

Battlefields at least admitted what they were.

This place looked as if someone had murdered a song.

There were fountains with no water, archways leading to floors that had fallen into the clouds, and white stone statues whose faces had been deliberately carved away. Feathers drifted through the air, but they were old and gray, crumbling before they touched the ground.

Diana stopped beside one broken statue.

For a while, she only stared.

"This was my teacher," she said.

The statue had no face.

I stood behind her and said nothing. Hey Ming lowered himself to the ground, making his huge body as small as he could. Even he understood that hunger had no place in this silence yet.

Diana reached out and brushed dust from the statue's wing.

"She told me angels were not born to be worshipped," Diana whispered. "She said we were born to keep light from becoming proud."

Her hand trembled.

"I forgot her voice until now."

The bond between us ached.

Not from injury.

From memory returning all at once.

She placed her hand on the trunk.

Her light flickered.

Not enough.

The tree needed life. More life than she had. More than I had. More than any ordinary creature could give.

Hey Ming stepped forward.

Diana turned sharply. "No."

He ignored her and lowered his head.

Black flame touched the fallen chains.

It did not burn the tree.

It ate the corruption.

For the first time, Hey Ming's hunger became gentle.

Silver Butterfly unfolded around the roots, channeling life crystals gathered from every battlefield we had survived. I poured my own power through the lifebound contract until my vision blurred.

Diana began to sing.

Gold light rose from the dead branches.

One leaf appeared.

Then another.

Then thousands.

The sacred tree bloomed beneath a sky washed clean.

Figures made of fading light appeared among the branches: not fully alive, not fully gone. Memories of angels who had fallen defending their home. They bowed to Diana.

One voice, vast and kind, spoke through the leaves.

"Child of the last branch, you return not alone."

Diana held my hand with both of hers.

"No," she said. "I return with family."

The tree released a seed of gold and silver.

It floated into her chest.

Diana's wings changed, gaining a second layer of moonlit feathers.

**Diana evolution complete.**

**Authority awakened: Sacred Realm Restoration.**

She cried then.

Quietly.

No stat card could explain that part.

## Final Hunt

Peace did not arrive all at once.

It came through work.

Border by border.

Dungeon by dungeon.

Mountain range by mountain range.

The Fallen Clan tried to regroup. Hey Ming found their hidden nests. The Shadow Congregation tried to raise another disaster ritual. Diana purified it before the first sacrifice died. Mutant beast kings tried to break through. Silver Butterfly cut them open and Hey Ming ate the rest.

Years later, people would tell the story badly.

They would say Chenlin was born lucky.

They would say Hey Ming had always been a world-ending wolf.

They would say Diana descended as a divine angel, and Silver Butterfly was a legendary weapon from the start.

They would forget the puppy in the summoning hall.

They would forget the laughter.

They would forget a poor student selling everything he owned for meat no one believed would matter.

That was fine.

Stories always polish away the dirt.

I remembered.

On the day Yunk Academy built a statue of Hey Ming at the front gate, I stood in the crowd wearing a formal uniform I hated. Luwan cried openly. Ji Fei pretended not to. Teacher Luen looked older, but when our eyes met, he bowed.

Lu Ru Yan stood at the edge of the plaza. She had survived the war, grown stronger, and become someone who no longer looked away from her choices.

She smiled once.

I nodded back.

Some debts do not need repayment.

Some endings do not need revenge.

My little sister stood near the front with both hands clasped around the railing, staring up at the statue until her neck must have hurt. When she saw me looking, she waved so hard that the formal sleeve of her academy-prep uniform nearly slapped the student beside her.

For a second, I saw the girl who had pressed a coin into my palm in a bare room and pretended not to cry.

Then I saw the future she had bought with it.

Not all of it, of course.

One coin did not buy victory.

But it had been the last weight that tipped a choice from impossible to possible. People liked to measure history in battles and breakthroughs. They forgot that sometimes a legend began because a child believed one more coin might matter.

Beside her, a framed portrait of my mother had been placed among the honored family seats. I had not asked who arranged it. Maybe Ji Fei. Maybe Luen. Maybe Yang Xiao, who had developed an irritating habit of doing kind things and pretending they were administrative necessities.

I bowed my head to the portrait.

Only for a moment.

Long enough.

Hey Ming, now small by choice, sat beside my boot in his puppy form and looked up at the enormous statue of himself.

He barked.

"Yes," I said. "They made you look too serious."

Diana giggled from my shoulder.

Silver Butterfly rested on my sleeve, wings folded like polished moonlight.

Yang Xiao approached with a tablet in hand and the expression of a man about to ruin my quiet morning.

"There is a new spatial disturbance," he said.

I sighed.

"How far?"

"Far."

"Dangerous?"

"Extremely."

Hey Ming's tail wagged.

Diana's eyes brightened.

Silver Butterfly unfolded one wing.

I looked at the academy gates, the students watching us with the same awe I once saw turned into mockery, and felt something settle in me.

Not pride.

Not satisfaction.

Direction.

The world was larger than the people who laughed at your beginning.

It was larger than the systems that ranked you too early.

It was larger than fear, larger than fate, larger than any gate that opened in the sky.

I picked up the puppy who had once been despised by everyone and held him at eye level.

"Ready?"

Hey Ming licked my face.

Some things never changed.

So I smiled, turned toward the waiting transport, and carried my impossible little monster into the next horizon.

The legend did not end with the devouring wolf saving Wooan.

That was only when the world finally understood what I had known from the beginning.

A useless summon does not exist.

Only a power no one has fed yet.
